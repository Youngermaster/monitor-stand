\section{Stability and safety}\label{sec:safety}

The printed parts are strong for a 2.0\,kg panel: the bending moment at the joints is about 1.5\,N·m. The real limit is
tipping, because a monitor on a stand has a high centre of mass. The table shows how hard you would have to push the
\emph{top edge} of the screen to start tipping the stand.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Push needed at the top edge of the screen to tip the stand (estimate).}
\zebra
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}Y L{2.6cm}L{2.6cm}L{2.6cm}@{}}
\toprule
\thd{Base and feet infill} & \thd{Portrait, forward} & \thd{Portrait, back} & \thd{Landscape, forward} \\
\midrule
45\% (normal print)        & 4.6\,N & 7.0\,N & 5.5\,N \\
100\% (recommended)        & 6.0\,N & 8.3\,N & 7.3\,N \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

That is plenty for normal use (adjusting, cleaning, a bump), but the stand is not meant to be pulled from the top.

\begin{itemize}
  \item Print the base and feet at 100\% and use the rubber bumpers, so it does not slide.
  \item Keep it at least a hand's width from the edge of the desk.
  \item PLA softens around 55\,°C: keep it out of direct sun and away from heaters.
  \item Check that the screws are snug once a month; PLA can creep a little under constant load.
  \item Designed for this 2.0\,kg panel. Re-check before mounting anything heavier.
\end{itemize}
