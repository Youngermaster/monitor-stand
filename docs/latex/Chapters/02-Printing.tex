\section{What to print}\label{sec:printing}

All six parts are in \code{stl/} and numbered in print order. Print everything in \textbf{white PLA+}. None of
them needs supports: every overhang is either a short bridge or a 45° chamfer.

\begin{nota}
\textbf{Why on its side?} The column and arm carry the monitor as a bending load. Printed on their side, every
layer runs along the full length of the part, which is several times stronger than stacking the layers along it.
\end{nota}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Layer height} 0.20\,mm, \textbf{top/bottom} 6 layers, nozzle 210--220\,°C for PLA+, bed 60\,°C.
  \item \textbf{Infill pattern} gyroid. A 5\,mm brim on the column and arm helps them stay flat.
  \item \textbf{Filament.} With the base and feet at 100\% the set uses about 1.9\,kg (two spools). At 60\% it is about 1.5\,kg.
  \item \textbf{Hole sizes.} Insert holes are 6.4\,mm, screw clearance holes 5.5\,mm (M5) and 4.6\,mm (M4). If your
        printer prints holes small, run a 6.4\,mm or 5.5\,mm drill through by hand.
\end{itemize}

\begin{aviso}
\textbf{Print the base and feet dense.} They are the counterweight. With 100\% infill the stand needs about
30\% more force to tip over than with a normal 45\% print (\cref{sec:safety}). If you print them lighter,
add the rubber pads and keep the stand away from the desk edge.
\end{aviso}
